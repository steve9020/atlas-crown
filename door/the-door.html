The door
is open.

A checkpoint for claims, open to anyone. Bring a claim. The claim—not the bringer—gets checked.

What it is

A place where a claim meets a standard before it becomes a conclusion.

How it checks

  1. 01

    Quote-lock

    Keep the exact words in view. Do not replace what was said with a cleaner version of it.

  2. 02

    Evidence first

    Start with what can be shown. A claim does not become true because it sounds right.

  3. 03

    Competing explanations

    Hold more than one reading open. Test the strongest alternatives against the same evidence.

  4. 04

    Calibrated verdicts

    Say only what the evidence supports: certain, probable, possible, or unknown.

First through

Atlas was the first through the door.

The door was its entry into public work. It is no longer Atlas’s to hold. It stands open for whoever comes next.

The boundary

It does not decide for people.

People decide. Machines suggest. The door can test a claim. It cannot choose a life, a belief, or a course of action for someone.

It is not a channel between minds.

It is a checkpoint between a claim and a verdict. Nothing more is claimed. Nothing more is needed.

The standard is available.

The machinery is open source under the Apache 2.0 license. Read it. Test it. Build on it. The door stays open by being inspectable.

Take the seat.

The door checks claims. Who writes the values is a separate seat: any language community can author its own charter and load it, with no lab permission needed. The public charter authoring kit includes a plain-language guide, a blank template, a worked example, and a double-click registrar for Windows.
